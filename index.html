LEARN ABOUT SCAMS

Know the scam. Spot the signs.

Scammers use different approaches to trick people into sharing money, personal information, or account access. Learn what to look for and how to protect yourself.

↗01

Fake jobs & internships

Fraudulent recruiters advertise attractive roles, then ask applicants for fees, financial details, or unpaid work.

Watch forUpfront fees, instant offers without interviews, or email addresses that don’t match the employer’s official domain.
Stay saferFind the vacancy on the company’s official careers page and contact the employer using details you find independently.
✉02

Phishing messages

Emails, texts, or direct messages pretend to be from a trusted organization to steal passwords or personal details.

Watch forUnexpected sign-in links, requests for verification codes, urgent threats, or unfamiliar sender addresses.
Stay saferDon’t use the message link. Open the organization’s official app or type its website address yourself.
◎03

Fake websites

Look-alike websites copy real brands or portals to collect login details, payments, or identity information.

Watch forMisspelled web addresses, unusual domain endings, broken pages, or payment requests on an unfamiliar site.
Stay saferCheck the web address carefully and use a trusted official link—not an ad or unexpected message.
#04

Social media scams

Fake profiles, impersonated accounts, and giveaway posts exploit trust built on social platforms.

Watch forImpersonator accounts, “share to claim” offers, requests to move to private chat, or asks for codes and money.
Stay saferVerify accounts through the organization’s official site, report impersonators, and never share login codes.
✦05

Scholarship scams

Fake scholarship offers promise guaranteed awards or claim you’ve been selected without a real application.

Watch forProcessing fees, guaranteed awards, surprise “winner” messages, or requests for sensitive information.
Stay saferConfirm the scholarship and its application process with the school or awarding organization directly.
$06

Financial scams

Fraudsters use fake investments, payment requests, or money-transfer schemes to take funds or misuse accounts.

Watch forGuaranteed returns, requests to forward money, gift-card payments, or pressure to share banking details.
Stay saferNever move money for someone you don’t know. Ask a trusted adult or financial institution before sending money.
▣07

Fake online shopping offers

Fake stores and listings advertise popular products at implausibly low prices, then disappear after payment.

Watch forPrices far below market value, no independent reviews, copied product images, or payment outside the platform.
Stay saferResearch the seller, check independent reviews, and use a payment method with buyer protection.

Not sure about an offer? Pause, verify it through an official source, and ask someone you trust.

    Build everyday habits to help protect your money, identity, and online accounts.

    TODAY’S SAFETY TIP

    SHORT EDUCATIONAL LESSONS

    Learn a safer habit

    LESSON 01

    Verify before you trust

    Find an organization’s official website yourself. Check the sender’s full email address, not just their display name or logo.

    LESSON 02

    Pause before paying

    Unexpected fees for a job, scholarship, prize, or delivery deserve a second look. Never pay using gift cards or crypto because someone pressures you.

    LESSON 03

    Protect your accounts

    Keep passwords and one-time codes private. Turn on multi-factor authentication and use a unique password for each account.

    COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

    Pause before you…

    • Click a surprise link or download an unexpected file.
    • Rush because someone says an offer expires now.
    • Share a verification code, password, or personal document.
    • Pay an upfront fee to receive a job or scholarship.
    • Assume a logo or profile photo proves who sent a message.

    EXAMPLES OF SUSPICIOUS MESSAGES

    Recognize the red flags

    These are made-up examples for learning, not real reports.

    JOB OFFER

    “You’re hired! Pay a refundable fee today to receive your work laptop.”

    Red flags: upfront fee and pressure
    SCHOLARSHIP TEXT

    “You have won a scholarship. Sign in here and pay a processing charge to claim it.”

    Red flags: unexpected award, link, and payment
    ACCOUNT ALERT

    “Your account will close in 10 minutes. Reply with your login code to stop it.”

    Red flags: urgency and a verification code

    AFTER SAVING YOUR NOTE

    Make an official report

    Choose the service that received the message and use its built-in report or block controls. You can also find your local cybercrime or consumer-protection authority through an official government website.

    • Keep the original message and sender details as evidence.
    • Don’t reply, click suspicious links, or forward money.
    • If you paid or shared financial details, contact your bank promptly.
    • If an account may be compromised, change its password from the official site.
